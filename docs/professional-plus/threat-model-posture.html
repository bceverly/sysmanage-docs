Threat Model and Posture

Tell SysManage what your installation is for and what you are worried about, and it tells you which installation-wide controls you are missing -- as a punch list you can work through, waive with a reason, or fix from the same screen.

Overview

The Advisor looks at hosts one at a time. Some of the most important controls are not properties of any one host: whether administrators must use MFA, whether logs leave the machines they describe, whether backups meet the recovery point you need. Posture checks those, for the installation as a whole.

Which of them matter depends on who you are. A home lab does not need FIPS-validated cryptography; a defense contractor does. So posture starts from a short threat model -- a questionnaire about your data, obligations and exposure -- and applies only the checks your answers call for.

The threat-model questionnaire

The questionnaire asks one question at a time, each with a short note on why it is asked. It covers what the installation is, the kinds of data it holds, the regulations that apply, who you are worried about, how reachable the systems are, who else has access, how many people administer them, and how much downtime, data loss and risk you can tolerate.

  • It branches. A question only appears when an earlier answer calls for it -- the regulations question only if you hold sensitive data, the FIPS question only for the public sector or CMMC. Closing a branch discards its answers, so an old answer can never quietly shape the model.
  • It opens on your current answers. Re-running it is an edit, not a fresh start.
  • Every save is a new version. The model is kept per tenant, and nothing is ever overwritten.
  • An incomplete model is shown as incomplete. The checks that depend on an unanswered question are not assessable -- never assumed to pass.

What is checked

The checks ship as the curated SysManage posture rule pack, so a tenant can switch the pack, or a single check, off under Rule packs. Depending on the threat model, they include:

  • MFA required for administrators, and enrolled by every administrator; account lockout after failed logins; sessions that expire; a minimum password length
  • Logs forwarded off the hosts, and audit history kept for at least a year
  • Secrets kept in OpenBAO, and no API key left without an expiry
  • Backups that meet your recovery point objective, alerting when something breaks, and changes that land in planned maintenance windows
  • Security updates on a schedule, and no host with an unpatched critical vulnerability
  • FIPS mode wherever it is available, antivirus and a firewall on every host, and a compliance scan from the last 30 days on every host
  • For air-gapped installations, a server role that matches the deployment you described

Checks that span the fleet report coverage: how many hosts pass, how many fail, and how many have not reported. A host that has not reported keeps the check from passing.

Four states, and only one of them is a pass

  • Satisfied -- the control is in place, on current evidence.
  • Open -- the control is missing. The item carries a risk score and a way to fix it.
  • Waived -- the control is missing and someone has accepted that, with a reason. It is shown in a neutral color, never as a pass: the risk was accepted, not removed.
  • Not assessable -- SysManage could not tell, and says why: the evidence could not be read, it has no view of this on this installation, some hosts have not reported, or the threat model is incomplete.

An item that was satisfied and is open again because the fleet changed is marked Regressed. An item that opens because you changed the threat model is not a regression, and is never reported as one.

Waivers

An administrator can waive an open item. A waiver needs a reason, and granting, re-affirming and revoking one are each written to the tamper-evident audit log. The item keeps evaluating underneath, so the waiver never hides what is actually true.

A waiver covers the risk as it stood when it was granted. It lapses -- and the item shows as open again, marked Waiver lapsed -- when the risk rises, when the check itself changes, or when the threat-model answers it was based on change. Re-affirming it, after a fresh look, restores it.

Fixing an item

Every check names how it is fixed, and Fix always shows a preview of what will change, and on which hosts, before anything does:

  • A setting -- requiring MFA for administrators, locking accounts after repeated failed logins, shortening sessions -- is changed from the dialog.
  • A fleet change -- enabling FIPS mode, the firewall or installed antivirus -- is queued for the affected hosts and applied in their maintenance windows.
  • Guided fixes need a decision only you can make, such as where logs should go or which backup command to run, so the dialog takes you to the screen where it is made.
  • Applying a fix requires an administrator, and also the role the equivalent per-host action needs, so the Posture tab never widens anyone's permissions. Every fix applied is audited.
  • On a multi-tenant installation, settings that belong to the whole server are marked Managed by the server operator, and a tenant cannot change them from here.

History and model versions

Every change to an item is recorded with its cause, and each item's history is one click away. When the threat model has more than one version, Compare shows what changed between two versions -- the risk factors that were added or removed -- and what that change did to the punch list.

  • GET /api/v1/advisor/threat-model/questionnaire -- the questionnaire
  • GET|POST /api/v1/advisor/threat-model -- the current threat model and its versions; save a new version
  • GET /api/v1/advisor/threat-model/diff -- what changed between two versions, and what it did to the punch list
  • GET /api/v1/advisor/posture -- the punch list
  • GET /api/v1/advisor/posture/history -- every change, classified, newest first
  • POST|DELETE /api/v1/advisor/posture/{rule}/waiver -- waive or revoke; .../waiver/reaffirm re-affirms
  • GET|POST /api/v1/advisor/posture/{rule}/remedy -- preview or apply the fix

Getting started

  1. Open Insights > Advisor and choose the Posture tab.
  2. Press Start the questionnaire and answer the questions. The punch list is filled in on the next evaluation, or press Evaluate now.
  3. Look at Not assessable items first: they are the places SysManage cannot see.
  4. Work through the Open items -- fix them, or waive the ones you accept, with a reason your auditor will understand.
