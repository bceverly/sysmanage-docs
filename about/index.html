SysManage is built by an engineering leader who spent three decades living the exact problem it solves — heterogeneous fleets, cybersecurity as an operational discipline, and systems management at global scale.

Bryan Everly — Founder

Engineering executive, cybersecurity practitioner, and lifelong systems builder. Master of Information & Cybersecurity, UC Berkeley. IBJ CTO of the Year.

SysManage didn't start as a business plan. It started as a conviction, formed over thirty years of shipping and operating infrastructure software: the operating system stopped being the right unit of management a long time ago. Real environments are heterogeneous — Windows next to a half-dozen Linux distributions, macOS, the BSDs, containers, virtualization, air-gapped networks — and the tools built to manage them never caught up.

That conviction is earned, not theoretical. At Canonical, I led the Ubuntu systems-management platform across five CPU architectures, serving millions of users. At Cummins I was Chief Enterprise Architect, wiring AI/ML and IoT into global manufacturing. As a CTO and CISO at Cox Automotive's NextGear Capital, I built a security organization into the DevOps pipeline and shipped machine-learning risk and fraud systems. Across ExactTarget, Aprimo, Alliance Data, and going back to a software company that IPO'd and was acquired by IBM, the through-line is the same: large fleets, mixed platforms, and the operational cost of managing them with a different tool for every silo.

SysManage is the platform I always wanted to hand my teams: one control plane that treats every operating system — including the BSDs that everyone else ignores — as a first-class citizen, with the security, compliance, and air-gap capabilities that regulated enterprises actually require. It's open-source at the core because infrastructure you can't inspect isn't infrastructure you should trust.
